# 2059 design bible

The rules of the game as they are built today. Every number here comes from the code; the file that owns each rule is named so the two can be kept in step. When a rule changes, change it here in the same commit.

Story and setting live in the owner's universe notes, which stay out of this public repo. This file covers mechanics only. In-game text never explains the world: maker blurbs on implant cards are what's stamped on the part ("Cleared for passage."), enemy descriptions say what you see and how it fights, and the title and death screen don't explain what the signal is. There are no hints: the board and the cards show how things work, and the player finds out the rest by playing (save version 15 dropped the part that remembered seen hints). Everything else, including chapter rules like tides, wind and the alarm, the player finds out by playing. Fight chips name a state ("Burning", "High tide") without saying what to do about it, and the enemy panel shows its health number but only bars for structure and heat.

## 1. Fights

**Where:** `client/src/lib/engine/fight.ts` (rules), `client/src/lib/stores/useCombat.tsx` (timing), `client/src/lib/combat/grid.ts` (arenas).

### Turn order
1. **Start of your turn.** You and the enemy each lose 6 heat. A host whose rules allow it (the Operative firmware at 25 heat or less, or the play-i Heat node) gets a **double action** this turn: after the first action, one more from where you stand (no second move; `moveSpent`). The log says so.
2. **Move.** Reach is measured round (a straight step costs 1, a diagonal about 1.41; `getPlayerReach`), so it makes a circle, not a box. Movement is an area of floor (`moveReach`, `MOVE_BASE_REACH` 1.5, `MOVE_AREA` 6.5): with no bonus you reach the 8 tiles around you, and each movement point (play-i Locomotion nodes, leg and Reflex upgrades) adds the same amount of floor, so reach grows slowly: 12 tiles at 1 point, 20 at 2, a full 5x5 (24) at 3, 28 at 4. Units and cover block the way. Tap your start tile to undo. At 100 heat you can't move.
3. **Act.** One action from your bar of four, or a stim, or pass. Overclock is the only stim that doesn't end your turn.
4. **Hazard.** If you end on a hazard tile, you take its damage.
5. **Parting blows.** Every enemy you started the turn beside, and walked away from (no longer beside you), hits you once for a 12-damage close-quarters blow, scaled by threat. A stunned enemy, one winding up a heavy, or one your attack killed doesn't. Move tiles are red when going there hurts (a parting blow, ending inside an enemy's reach, or a hazard underfoot) and blue when it's safe.
6. **Enemy turn.** Each enemy in turn fires a wound-up heavy, or **attacks from where it stands** with a move that reaches (a shield, heavy wind-up or ordinary attack), **then walks** up to 2 tiles (3 if Fast). With nothing in reach it only walks. It walks like a hunter: of the tiles it can reach it ends where its attacks cover its victim's tile and as many of the tiles the victim could step to as it can (avoiding hazards), or closes in the usual way when nothing covers the victim yet. Then it takes hazard damage if it's standing on one.

### Several enemies
- A level can throw 1–3 enemies at you (random for now; the story will set it later). The first level, every 5th level (elites, bosses) and the first-life Enforcer are always alone.
- Groups are scaled down: two enemies each have 60% health and 70% attack, three have 45% and 55%. Extra enemies are drawn from this level's type and the two before it.
- **Aiming:** picking an attack aims it at a tile automatically (the aim that hits the most enemies; the enemy you last looked at, then the nearest, win ties). Faint outlines show every tile it can be aimed at; the bright tiles are where it lands, and "Hit" shows over each enemy in them. Tap another outlined tile to aim there. **Every enemy on the bright tiles is hit**, each with its own damage roll; there's no target to pick first. Tapping an enemy outside the attack's reach just shows its details.
- **Arm moves have a side:** a left-arm implant's move only aims at tiles level with or left of you, a right-arm move level with or right of you (straight up and down work for both). Its button says "◀ left side" or "right side ▶".
- **Enemy turn:** each living enemy acts in turn and treats the others as obstacles. A guard soaks one hit, so a crowd can't all hit a braced player at full guard.
- The fight is won when the last enemy falls.

### Arenas
- **Every fight room has its own drawn arena** (`arena` in `map-indian-ocean.json`, built by `buildArena` in `grid.ts`), the same on every run so a place is a place. Text rows: `.` deck, `~` open water, `#` crates, `d` drums, `n` nets, `p` pumps, `?` a loose crate (there half the time, seeded per fight), `f` fuel fire, `c` live cable, `b` chemical bilge, `h` split decking, `P` your start, `E` enemy starts (the first is the lead; extra enemies start near the E tiles). Sizes run from 5 to 9 wide and 7 to 13 tall.
- **Location types** give rooms their feel: raft clusters (islands and 1-wide bridges over water), gantries (tall and narrow, long open lines), container yards and holds (mazes of cover), foundries and smelters (fire channels), hulls and pump rooms (wide, pillars), the chapel (symmetric, open centre), ambush pockets (the barge and archive: you start near enemies on two sides), and boss stages (the throne hall, the foundry floor, the ring of deck round the drowned spire's water).
- The content check rejects arenas with ragged rows, unknown characters, no or several P, no E, or an E the player can't walk to; every fight room must have one.
- Farm fights (no room) still roll an arena: portrait, 7x9 growing with movement and bodies up to 9x13, one of four outlines (open, corridor, L-shape, cross) with scattered cover and hazards.
- **Open water:** a body knocked into the sea takes collision damage (30% of the hit, at least 4) but doesn't drown. While it's in the water it can't be hit or targeted, makes no threat and draws no parting blow; on its next turn it climbs out onto the nearest open deck tile, and that's its whole turn. The player in the sea is left alone by enemies and spends their next turn climbing out.

### Attack shapes
Each shape has one job and one way to beat it (`client/src/lib/combat/patterns.ts`). Player moves use the same shapes.

| Shape | Hits | Beat it by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strike (`melee`) | the one adjacent tile you're on | not ending next to it |
| Reach (`melee_long`) | a straight line of 2 | leaving its row or column |
| Lunge | only the tile exactly 2 away, any of 8 directions | standing adjacent, or 3 away |
| Sweep (`sweep_arc`) | a 3-tile arc in front | getting behind or beside it |
| Cone | a wedge along a row or column: 1 tile, then 3 | getting to its side |
| Shot (`ranged`) | one tile 2–4 away on a row, column or diagonal, with no cover, void or body in between | breaking the line |
| Knockback | a straight line of up to 3; the one hit is pushed 1 tile straight back if there's room | keeping your back off hazards |
| Charge | a lane of 3 toward the target; the attacker dashes down it, stopping short of anything in the way | leaving the lane |
| X-burst (`diagonal_cross`) | all four diagonals, 2 deep | standing square to it |
| Ring | every tile exactly 2 away | hugging it or backing off |
| Beam | the whole row or column toward the target, to the edge | changing row or column |
| Blast (`aoe`) | a 3×3 square centred on a tile up to 3 away | leaving the square |

**Hitting several at once:** an attack hits the enemies standing on the tiles it lands on. For the wide shapes (`MULTI_HIT`: sweep, cone, blast, lob, burst, ring, X-burst, pierce, beam) how many depends on the implant's **targeting part** (see Implant parts): Focused hits one, Split 2 or 3 that many, Wide everyone in it. A capped move picks the enemies on its strongest tiles first, then the nearest. Cards say "Focused: hits one, 25% harder", "Hits up to 3 in it" or "Hits every enemy in it"; the fight bar shows ×2 or ×3 when an aim catches more than one. Firmware attacks and signature parts hit everyone in their shape.

**Sweet spots** (`tileWeight` in `patterns.ts`, `sweetSpot` in `fight.ts`): every shape lands harder on some of its tiles than others (the player's attacks): Reach ×1.15 at its tip and ×0.85 close; Cone ×1.2 at the point, ×0.95 down the middle, ×0.7 at the corners; Blast ×1.3 at its heart, ×0.75 round it; Lob ×1.2 / ×0.8; Shot ×1.1 at 2–3 tiles, ×0.85 at 4; Bolt ×1.1 to 2 tiles, ×0.85 at 4; Pierce weakens 10% a body; Long shot ×1.15 at 4+ tiles, ×0.85 closer; Sweep ×1.15 in the middle, ×0.8 at the ends; Knockback ×1.15 / 1 / ×0.85 by distance; Charge ×0.8 / 1 / ×1.2 (momentum); Grapple ×0.85 close, ×1.05 further; Beam ×1.1 close, ×0.8 far; Burst ×0.85 on the diagonals; X-burst ×1.1 close; Strike, Lunge and Ring hit evenly. The attack pictures and the aimed tiles on the board are shaded by how hard the move lands there (sweet spot × side or back bonus): the bolder the tile, the harder the hit. Moves that shove what they hit show an arrow pointing away from you on each tile, moves that drag show one pointing toward you, and dashes show a cyan dot where you land.

**Enemy attacks work the same way** (`resolveFoeAction`, `foeAct`, `dangerWeights`): an enemy's shape has the same sweet spots, measured from where it aimed (its victim, or for a heavy the tile it locked onto), so where you stand inside its reach decides how hard it lands: hug a reach or a charge and it's softer, stand at the tip and it's harder. A wide enemy attack (`MULTI_HIT`) hits everyone standing in it, you and your allies alike, not just the one it aimed at (the same blow: it heats the attacker once). The red zone is shaded by how hard their normal attacks would land on each tile: bolder red, harder hit.

### Enemy behaviour
- **Heavy attacks lock their tiles.** The enemy winds up where it stands, aimed at where its target stands; the orange tiles are exactly the tiles that fire next turn. Anyone on them then is hit (allies included), anyone off them is safe, wherever they are. If the shape can't reach its target from there, the heavy isn't wound up. Enemies only use heavies when their own heat is **50 or more** (see Heat).
- **Collisions:** a knockback whose next tile is solid (cover, the arena edge, another body) doesn't move its target; instead the target slams into it for 30% of the hit again (at least 4) and is **stunned**. A body it slams into takes the same damage.
- **Stunned:** loses its next turn (an enemy also loses any heavy it was winding up; a stunned ally sits out the round).
- **Burning:** ending a turn on a fuel fire sets you (or an enemy) burning for 2 turns: 4% of max health each turn (at least 3), at the start of that unit's turn. A flame and circling stars show over units that are burning or stunned.
- **Overload:** a meter (0–100, `OVERLOAD`) that fills as blows are traded and as you run hot: 25 × (damage you deal ÷ the target's max health) per hit, 40 × (damage you take ÷ your max health), and +10 for each turn you end Hot (+16 Critical). **It carries from fight to fight** for the same host (`overload` in the map state, like health; resting keeps it), and a new host starts empty. When full and an enemy is within 3 tiles, **Signal Surge** replaces the meter: every enemy within 3 tiles takes 1.5× the strongest move on your fight bar (precise), your heat vents to 0, and it uses your turn. It comes about once every three fights. (Each firmware's own Overload ultimate is for the firmware conversation.)
- **Speed and the turn clock** (`SPEED`, `DRIVE`, `startClock`, `nextUp`, `spendTurn`, `forecast` in fight.ts): turns come off a clock, not in rounds, so faster bodies act more often. After each turn a body waits `ROUND × 10 ÷ speed` (ROUND = 100): speed 10 acts once per 100, speed 13 every 77, speed 6 every 167. So a speed-13 enemy gets about four turns to a speed-10 host's three, and a slowed host (6) can see a hasted enemy act twice in a row. Ties go to the player, then enemies in their order, then allies. **Allies take their own turns** on the same clock at their own speed (`speed` in allies.json: Bale 8, Ward 9, Matron, Ninety and Dust 10, Breaker and Tally 11, Ghostline 12; actors `ally0`, `ally1`).
  - Speed = the class's base speed (Unclassified 10, Enforcer 8, Operative 11, Courier 13, Foreman 9, Splice 10, Wretch 10) + the average drive of the body's implants + firmware levels: Volkov +3; IOA and Kizuna +2; Tianxia, CBN and Crown +1; Patchwork 0; PARSU −1; Helix −2. No implants means 0. Enemies use the implants they carry, and Fast adds +1. At 85 heat or more, −2.
  - **Slowed** (−4) and **Hasted** (+4) last 2 rounds of clock time. A turn's wait is set by the speed the body has as that turn ends, so a Rush haste speeds up your very next turn. They come from the Sap mod (slows everyone you hit; Kizuna, PARSU, Patchwork) and the Rush mod (hastes you when you hit; Volkov, IOA, Patchwork), and from the Cadre's Storm Liturgy, the Warden's Shock Baton and the Listener's Static Cross, which slow you. Stun still costs a whole turn.
  - Everything that works "per round" (tides, the alarm, orders, Slowed and Hasted counting down) ticks once every 100 of clock time. Everything that works "per turn" (cooling, burning, bleeding, wind) happens on that body's own turns, so a fast host also cools more often.
  - Everyone's first turn comes half a wait in, so anything faster than you opens the fight ("They move first"). Your opening turn skips the start-of-turn cooling, as before.
  - The turn strip (top right of the board) shows who is acting, then the next five turns as things stand; a fast enemy shows up more than once. If an enemy will act twice before your next turn, the HUD warns ("acts twice"). Status shows your speed; implant cards show each maker's speed push.
- **Enemy side effects** (`effects` on a move in `enemies.json`: knock, pull, bleed, stun chance): the Boarder's Crane Hook and the Enforcer's Collector's Hook are grapples that drag you to them; the Cadre's Rib-Graft Ram, the Volkov Operator's Hydraulic Drive and the Fitter's Piston Drive knock you back (into cover, hazards or the sea); the Ghost-MIL's Signal Bleed is a long shot across the board that makes you bleed, and the Raider's Catheter Blade bleeds too; the Surgeon's Sedative Dart is a bolt with a 25% stun, Tianxia's Neural Disrupt 20%, the Unregistered Operator's Vestibular Strike 30%; IOA's Transit Denial and the Listener's Carrier Wave pierce; the Listener's Undertow drags everyone it hits toward it. Bleeding on you works like an enemy's: 5% of max health (at least 3) at the start of your next 2 turns. Being dragged stops at bodies, cover and water.
- **Purple-edged red tiles** are where an enemy's hit also drags or shoves you.
- **Signature heavies:** Scavenger charge, Boarder cone, Cadre blast, Ghost-MIL X-burst, Raider ring, Surgeon strike (a grab), IOA knockback, Tianxia beam, Warden knockback, Volkov charge, Enforcer ring, Unregistered beam.
- **Attack pictures** (`Footprint` in `hud.tsx`, from `attackFootprint` in `fight.ts`): every attack card shows a 7x7 board with you in the middle: every tile the move can hit, coloured by its damage type (amber physical, violet special, blended between, the same as the aimed tiles on the board), brighter where it lands harder on an enemy standing there (from the side +15%, from behind +30%: the flank bonus; the enemy is below you on the board). Arm moves aim only on their own side, as in a fight (a wide swing can still spill across). The range band in the damage line is only used by moves with a `rangeOptimal`, and no move has one today.
- **The red zone is a promise:** it shows every tile each enemy's normal attacks (and its close-quarters strike) can hit from where it stands. Because enemies attack before they move, ending your turn outside the red means no normal attack can reach you this enemy turn (other enemies don't count as cover here, since they may move first). Heavies show only as their locked orange tiles.
- **Grades:** enemies show how upgraded they are: Raw (threat 1–4), Grafted (5–9), Augmented (10–19), Chromed (20–29), Ascended (30+). The target card shows the grade and how many implants they carry (1 up to threat 5, 2 up to 10, then 3); in a fight the HUD names the implants, and each glows on the sprite in the grade's colour.
- **Implant traits:** each implant an enemy carries gives a trait by body slot (`ENEMY_TRAITS` in `actions.ts`): eyes **Sighted** (gains an optic shot at 80% of its usual hit if it has no gun), legs **Fast** (+1 movement), arms **Armed** (+10% damage), lungs **Enduring** (heals 2% of max health at the start of its turn; 1% for a boss, so a long boss fight can't stall; `ENDURING` in `fight.ts`), brain **Focused** (heavies from 30 heat instead of 45), ears **Alert** (no flanking bonus against it), nose **Filtered** (hazards don't hurt it), spine **Braced** (takes 10% less damage). The fight HUD names the target's traits.
- **Salvage:** implant drops come from the body slots the beaten enemies carried first (at the fight's rarity), then any slot.
- **Lines, sparingly:** at the start of a fight the lead enemy may say one line at the walking corpse (`lines` in `enemies.json`; `lateLines` join once 5 or more hosts are lost). Elites, bosses, set pieces and the first-life fight always speak (`isNotable`: every chapter's cast lists them); an ordinary enemy speaks the first time its kind is met, then one fight in six (`ORDINARY_LINE_CHANCE`). Only notable enemies leave the signal's first impression (`firstSight`) in the journal (`client/src/lib/stores/useJournal.tsx`). The Self page's Noticed panel groups the journal into Places, People and Allies, newest three of each with the rest on request; older impressions of ordinary enemies stay saved but aren't shown. Lines are flavour and use `Math.random`, so they don't change seeded runs. The line (and the signal's note) holds the fight: the screen dims, a card shows what was said, and nothing moves, not even an enemy that acts first, until the player taps to continue (`fightIntro`, `dismissIntro`).
- **Shields** come out only when the enemy is under half health, and then 35% of the time.
- Charges and lunges need a gap of 2 or more.
- An enemy right next to you with nothing that reaches falls back to a 12-damage close-quarters strike.

## 2. Damage

**Where:** `calculateDamage` and `estimateDamage` in `client/src/lib/combat/actions.ts`.

**Two kinds of damage, two bars.** Every move is a mix of **physical** (fists, blades, rams, kicks, bullets) and **special** (lasers, neural, EMP, thermal): `physicalRatio` is the physical share. Physical uses the attacker's **Attack** against the target's **Defence**; special uses **Sp. Atk** against **Sp. Def** (stored in the `structuralAttack` / `structuralDefense` fields). Each stat counts only for its share of the mix (×`STAT_MIX` 1.7, so a 50/50 move still draws well on both).

- **Structure soaks physical damage first**, like armour: the physical share comes off the structure bar until it's empty, and only what gets through reaches health. At zero structure, implants **malfunction** (see accuracy).
- **Special damage goes past structure, straight to health**, cut by Sp. Def, and **heats the target**: one degree for every 3 special damage, at most 15 (`heatFromSpecial`), so special builds feed overheating and burnout.
- Enemies work the same way, with their own structure bars and both stat pairs.
- Every class has a structure bar of its own before implants (`baseStructure`); implants and upgrades add to it.

```
physical = (move damage + Attack × weight × 1.7) × physical share, cut by Defence (+ guard)
special  = (move damage + Sp. Atk × weight × 1.7) × special share, cut by Sp. Def
cut      = × 40 ÷ (40 + defence)
× side or back bonus × heat multiplier × crit (×1.75) × sweet spot × accuracy roll   (at least 3 in all)
physical → structure first, the rest to health;  special → health, and +heat
```

- **Stat weight:** the player's stats count at **60%**; enemy stats count in full.
- **Defence:** 40 defence halves damage. Brace adds its guard to Defence for the next enemy hit.
- **CBN grafts** (`bypass_sdef`) make your special damage ignore the enemy's Sp. Def.
- **Colours:** moves show their mix as a colour (amber physical, violet special, blended) on their cards, attack pictures and the aimed tiles on the board, and as words ("Physical", "70% physical", "Special").

- **Where Sp. Atk and Sp. Def come from** (`MAKER_SPECIAL`, `SLOT_SPECIAL`, `specialLean`, `specialShare` in `augments.ts`): every implant splits its attack and defence between physical and special by its maker's character, nudged by its slot. Special share: Kizuna 80%, IOA 70%, Crown 60%, Patchwork and Helix 50%, CBN 40%, Tianxia 30%, Volkov and PARSU 20%; brain, eyes and ears +20%, arms and legs −20%. `items.json` is written this way and the content check holds every implant to it (within a point). An implant's upgrade tree splits its attack and defence nodes the same way (`getTreeStatBonuses`), and some nodes give Sp. Atk or Sp. Def straight (Signal edge, Neural gain, Shield lattice; capstones Overmind and Null field; Apex adds +6 Sp. Def).
- **Implants fade with the structure bar** (`liveStats`, `IMPLANT_FADE` in `actions.ts`; `implantStats` on a character): what implants and their trees add to Attack, Defence, Sp. Atk and Sp. Def works in full at full structure and falls in a straight line to half at none (on top of malfunction's aim). The player and allies have it; enemies don't. Damage previews and every hit use the live stats.
- **Enemy drops follow how they fight** (`rollCarried` with a `lean`): an enemy whose moves are mostly special carries mostly special-leaning implants, so what you strip off it follows what it hit you with. (Its own stats from those implants keep its old split.)

**Stat lines** (`CLASS_DEFINITIONS`; health / structure / Attack / Defence / Sp. Atk / Sp. Def / Speed): Unclassified 120 / 30 / 14 / 5 / 12 / 5 / 10; Enforcer 150 / 40 / 17 / 9 / 8 / 5 / 8; Operative 110 / 25 / 12 / 4 / 20 / 7 / 11; Courier 105 / 25 / 15 / 4 / 12 / 5 / 13; Foreman 135 / 45 / 15 / 7 / 12 / 8 / 9; Splice 95 / 20 / 10 / 5 / 14 / 9 / 10; Wretch 80 / 15 / 10 / 3 / 10 / 3 / 10. **Firmware loaded is a massive upgrade** (`FIRMWARE_LIFT` in `actions.ts`): with any firmware, the whole body (base line, implants and trees) runs at ×1.45 health, ×1.4 attack, ×1.25 defence and ×1.4 structure; Unclassified gets none of it. The Self page shows all seven and where speed comes from ("Speed 13: 13 base +1 implants +2 firmware"); the death screen shows each class's line. Firmware attacks have their own mix: Hydraulic Slam 90% physical, Precision Shot 30% physical (mostly special), Graft Whip half and half, the rest 70–75% physical.

### Accuracy (share of the rolled damage that lands)

| Tier | Range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Precise | 100% | Implant moves that are precise cost 3 extra heat |
| Variable | 70–95% | The default |
| Unreliable | 50–100% | Can match a precise hit on a good roll |
| Malfunction | 25–60% | Replaces your accuracy while your structure bar is at zero |

Enemy evasion lowers the bottom of the range by 1.5% per point (up to 30%), except for precise hits.

### Worked example
Strike (10 damage, 70% physical, variable) from a player with 12 Attack and 5 Sp. Atk, at warm heat, against an enemy with 6 Defence and 4 Sp. Def, face on:

- Physical: (10 + 12 × 0.6 × 1.7) × 0.7 = 15.6 → 15; × 40/46 = 13; × 1.1 warm = 14; rolled 70–95% → **9 to 13**, off its structure first
- Special: (10 + 5 × 0.6 × 1.7) × 0.3 = 4.5 → 4; × 40/44 = 3; × 1.1 = 3; rolled → **2**, to health (too little to heat it)

## 3. Heat

Heat is the game's core: something to ride, not avoid. **Where:** `getHeatPhase` and `getHeatMultipliers` in `client/src/lib/combat/types.ts`; `HEAT`, `HOT_FX`, `hotAction`, `burnout` in `fight.ts`.

| Heat | Band | Your damage | Damage you take | Crit chance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–35 | Cool | ×1.00 | ×1.00 | 0% |
| 36–60 | Warm | ×1.10 | ×1.05 | 8% |
| 61–85 | Hot | ×1.25 | ×1.15 | 18% |
| 86–100 | Critical | ×1.50 | ×1.40 | 30% |

- Actions add their own heat (Strike +11; brace −25; Helix braces −15 to −18). Standing next to the enemy when you attack adds +2. Everyone sheds 6 at the start of each of their turns.
- **Hot moves** (61+): every attack gains its maker's extra effect (`HOT_FX`): Volkov knocks back and crushes, Tianxia knocks back, Kizuna shoots through cover, Crown heals 25% of the damage, CBN makes them bleed, IOA has a 25% stun, PARSU crushes and knocks back, Patchwork sets them alight. Moves with no maker (Strike, guns) push 4 more heat into the target instead. Firmware attacks count as their maker (Enforcer Volkov, Operative Kizuna, Courier IOA, Foreman Tianxia). The log says "HOT" when it happens.
- **Venting is a weapon:** bracing from 55 heat or more lets the heat out as a burst on every enemy beside you: 0.06 damage per degree vented per point of your physical attack, cut by its defence.
- **Burnout at 100:** you take 12% of your max health, a 50-degree burst hits everyone beside you, your heat drops to 50 and you lose your next turn (Runaway keeps the turn). Heat pushed onto you by an enemy can tip you over too.
- **Cooking enemies:** an enemy pushed to 100 heat (by your hits' heat transfer, or its own attacks) overheats: it takes 10% of its max health, is stunned (losing any heavy it was winding up) and drops to 50.
- **Enemies wind up heavies from their own heat** (50 or more; Focused ones from 30), whatever yours is.
- Acting twice when cool is no longer for everyone: it's the Operative's rule (25 or less), and the play-i Heat node can give it to anyone (25 or less).

## 4. Implants

**Where:** `client/src/lib/combat/augments.ts`, data in `client/src/content/items.json`.

- **10 slots.** Active: left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg, eyes. Passive: brain, ears, nose, lungs, spine.
- **Active implants** each give one combat move, built from the slot (arm, leg or eyes), the maker and the rarity. Damage scales ×1.00 / 1.20 / 1.45 / 1.75 / 2.10 from common to legendary. Helix active implants give braces, never attacks.
- **Passive implants** give stats and always-on effects only.
- **Maker** is read from the `[MAKER]` prefix in the item's name.

### Power bands
Each implant's stats are scaled so its power score lands near its rarity's target: common 4, uncommon 8, rare 14, epic 22, legendary 34. Power = attack + defence + health ÷ 4 + structural attack + structural defence + 2 × evasion. Bands never overlap (tested).

### Condition
Worn 80%, degraded 60%, corroded 45% of the implant's stats. Condition never makes a stat negative. Degraded parts cost 2 max health, corroded parts 5.

### Structure bar
The sum over installed implants of **slot size × maker toughness × rarity × condition**, plus upgrade defence.

| Slot | Size | | Maker | Toughness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arms, legs | 12 | | Helix | 1.5 |
| Spine | 11 | | PARSU | 1.4 |
| Lungs | 6 | | Volkov | 1.35 |
| Eyes, brain | 4 | | Tianxia | 1.3 |
| Ears, nose | 3 | | Patchwork | 1.0 |
| | | | IOA, Crown | 0.9 |
| | | | CBN | 0.8 |
| | | | Kizuna | 0.6 |

Rarity multiplies structure by 1.00 / 1.15 / 1.30 / 1.50 / 1.75. Brace repairs 40% of the bar; a Structural Patch repairs 60%. At zero you **malfunction** (see accuracy).

### Health cost
CBN parts cost 5 max health, Volkov and IOA 3, scaled by rarity (×1.00 to ×1.75), plus any condition cost. Max health never drops below **30**.

### Implant parts
Every implant is built from parts when it drops, like guns in Borderlands (`rollParts` and `rollPassiveParts` in `augments.ts`, seeded). Two drops with the same name can fight differently. Helix arms and legs are braces and have no parts.

**Active implants (arms, legs, eyes) have seven parts:**

1. **Frame**: the maker (base damage, heat, structure, health cost).
2. **Head**: the attack's shape.
3. **Lens** (`LENSES`): how it aims. Clear lens = precise (always full damage, +3 heat); Stock = variable; Cracked = unreliable but 12% harder. Wide shapes' lens also carries the **targeting part** (below).
4. **Coupling** (`COUPLINGS`): how the force reaches them, which sets the damage type (see Damage). Hydraulic 90% physical, Servo 70%, Arc 40%, Thermal 15% (and pushes 3 more heat into them).
5. **Core** (`CORES`): power against heat. Cold core 10% softer, 4 less heat; Stock; Hot core 15% harder, 5 more heat; Heat pump pushes 5 more heat into the target for 2 more of your own. Half of all cores are stock.
6. **Mods**: side effects.
7. **Quirk**: a rare rule (epic and legendary only).

Lens, coupling and core lean toward the maker's own 60% of the time (`MAKER_LEAN`): Volkov clear / hydraulic / hot; Tianxia stock / hydraulic / stock; Kizuna clear / arc / cold; Crown clear / arc / cold; CBN stock / servo / hot; IOA cracked / thermal / pump; PARSU stock / hydraulic / stock; Patchwork cracked / servo / pump. They are rolled after the head and mods, so a seed rolls the same head and mods as before they existed. Implants from before keep their maker's old accuracy, mix and heat. The parts line on cards names every part ("Hook head · Split 3 targeting (rare) · Clear lens · Arc coupling · Hot core · Bleed").

**Passive implants (brain, ears, nose, lungs, spine) have three:**

- **Housing** (`HOUSINGS`): Light (structure ×0.75, +2 evasion), Stock, Plated (structure ×1.35), Sealed (Sp. Def +3 on a common, growing with grade to +8 on a legendary; `partsSpDef`).
- **Regulator** (`REGULATORS`): Stock; Heat sink (shed 1 more heat a turn); Baffle (special hits heat you half as much); Primer (start each fight 10 hotter).
- **Trait** (`TRAITS`; rare 35%, epic 60%, legendary always): Tough (+8 health), Shielded (+4 Sp. Def), Quick (+1 speed), Charged (+15 Overload at the start of each fight), Shod (hazards hurt 25% less).
- Makers lean: Volkov plated / primer; Tianxia plated / sink; Kizuna light / baffle; Crown sealed / sink; CBN light / primer; IOA light / primer; PARSU plated / baffle; Patchwork stock / stock; Helix sealed / sink. Regulators and traits become fight perks (`partPerks`); housing and Tough change stats. Spliced parts count too. Passives from before have none.

| Quality | Heads | Mods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common | basic heads only (these include wide ones: Hook, Spread, Sweep, Shock Stomp, Lance) | 0 |
| Uncommon | basic heads | 0 or 1 (half the time) |
| Rare | any non-signature head | 1 |
| Epic | any non-signature head | 2 |
| Legendary | half the time the maker's signature head, else any | 2 (signatures carry their own effects) |

- **Heads** (damage multiplier): arms jab ×1.0, drive (reach 2) ×0.95, lash (exactly 2) ×1.1 [basic]; hook (arc) ×0.85, cutter (X-burst) ×0.8, spread (cone) ×0.85, grapple (line of 3, pulls) ×0.8. Legs stomp ×1.0, kick (knockback line) ×0.9, lunge ×1.05 [basic]; charge ×1.0, sweep ×0.85, step-cut (jab, then step back) ×0.95. Eyes bolt ×1.0, scope (2–4 shot) ×1.05 [basic]; lance (pierce) ×0.9, mortar (lob) ×0.8.
- **Signatures** (legendary): Volkov Breach Line (charge lane, dash through, knockback), Tianxia Crowd Press (arc, pushes all back), Kizuna Monowire (pierce through cover), Crown Bedside Manner (jab, drain 50%, vents 10 heat), CBN Harvest (grapple, pull, drain 40%, bleed), IOA Override Pulse (lob, 60% stun), PARSU Ground Slam (burst, crush), Patchwork Scrapstorm (cone, bleed, knockback); any maker's legs Sprint Strike (charge, dash), any maker's eyes Long Shot.
- **The targeting part** (`TARGETING`, `rollTargeting` in `augments.ts`; `parts.targeting`): every implant with a wide shape has one more part deciding how many enemies in the shape it hits, and what that costs. It rolls its own quality: the implant's, one step worse (30%) or one step better (30%), so a common sweep can carry a Split 3 part (an early crowd weapon) and a rare blast a Focused one (a pinpoint heavy hitter). **Focused** (common): one target, +25% damage, and no tile of its shape lands below full strength (its sweet spot never works against it), so it's easy to line up. **Split 2** (uncommon): up to 2, normal damage. **Split 3** (rare): up to 3, −10%. **Wide** (epic and better): everyone in it, −20%. Single-tile shapes (strike, lunge, shots) and signature heads don't take one. Implants from before targeting parts hit everyone, as they did. The parts line on cards names it ("Hook head · Split 3 targeting (rare)").
- **Leg moves mix moving and hitting:** besides Stomp, Kick, Lunge, Charge, Sweep, Step-cut and Sprint Strike, legs roll Spin Kick (sweep that knocks back), Shock Stomp (hits all eight tiles round you, weaker), Heel Drop (a 25% stun), Hook Kick (lunge that drags them in), Pounce (lunge that lands you beside them), Vault Kick (strike, then leap over them to their far side, ready to hit from behind), Slide Tackle (a dash down the lane that slows) and Drop Kick (lunge that knocks back). Makers lean on their own: Volkov Drop Kick; Tianxia Shock Stomp and Spin Kick; Kizuna Vault and Pounce; Crown Heel Drop; CBN Hook Kick and Pounce; IOA Slide Tackle and Vault; PARSU Shock Stomp and Heel Drop. A leap (Pounce, Vault) shows a cyan ring on the attack picture.
- **Mods:** Knockback (push every enemy hit a tile), Crush (knockback collisions ×2), Bleed (5% of max health, at least 3, at the start of its next 2 turns), Stun 25%, Drain (heal 25% of damage dealt), Coolant (vent 8 heat), Ignores cover. Makers carry their own: Volkov knockback/crush, Tianxia knockback/coolant, Kizuna cover/coolant, Crown drain/coolant, CBN bleed/drain, IOA stun/cover, PARSU crush/knockback, Patchwork any.
- **Quirks** (`QUIRKS` in `augments.ts`, rules in `fight.ts`: `quirkDamage`, `QUIRK`): rare parts that change a rule, not a number. Epic implants roll one 15% of the time, legendary 40% (stored with the mods, in place of the last one). **Sets them alight**: everyone hit catches fire (burning for 2 turns). **Runs hot**: +50% damage while you're at 60 heat or more. **Desperate**: double damage while you're under 30% health. **Reaper**: a takedown vents all your heat and guards you up (at least 10).
- A head picks from its maker's usual heads 60% of the time (Volkov reach, jab, spread, charge, kick, scope, lance; Tianxia jab, hook, kick, stomp, bolt, scope; Kizuna cutter, lash, lunge, step-cut, scope, lance; Crown jab, reach, sweep, step-cut, scope, bolt; CBN lash, grapple, lunge, kick, bolt, lance; IOA spread, hook, charge, kick, mortar, bolt; PARSU jab, hook, stomp, kick, bolt, mortar).
- The move's name is the part's **model number** plus the head (`modelOf` in `augments.ts`): each maker stamps its line its own way, and the grade rises with quality while the rest comes from the part, so a part always carries the same number. Crown RX-1 to RX-10 ("RX-2 Counter"), Volkov VK-1 to VK-10, Kizuna KZ-7A, Tianxia TX-100 to TX-540, Helix HX-10 to HX-55, CBN CB-10 to CB-24, IOA IO-11 to IO-55X, PARSU PS-1.0 to PS-5.9, Patchwork MK-1 to MK-7. Signature heads keep their own names. Long names wrap to two lines on the fight bar. **Every implant's name leads with the same model number** (`displayName`, used everywhere an implant is named: the body page, the bag, drops, the Grey Lane, allies, an enemy's implant list, event rewards), so two parts with one name are told apart at a glance and a move matches its implant. Signature implants and stims carry none. Names drop the (L)/(R) tag (the slot says the side) and wrap instead of being cut off; the body page's slot cards wrap to two lines at most so they never overlap. The card shows the parts, and every attack button shows a small footprint picture: you in cyan, where it can reach faint, where it lands bright.
- **What a move does is shown, not explained** (after Into the Breach: clarity first), so movesets can be compared between fights and mid-fight. On the board: aimed tiles take the damage-type colour and are bolder where it lands harder; the border shows the lens (a white inner line when it always lands in full, a broken border when it can roll low); "Hit" floats over only the enemies the aim will actually hit (kept inside the board). **Each struck enemy's health bar flashes the health this hit will take** (`estimateHit` in `fight.ts`, `healthMin`/`healthMax` from `estimateDamage`: bright for what it will surely take, faint for what it might; physical damage soaked by structure doesn't flash). **Who moves** shows as a bold arrow on the edge between a unit's tile and where it will go, nudging that way: white on each enemy it will shove or drag, cyan on you when it carries you (a dash or leap toward the aim, a step back away from it). If something is in the way (the edge, cover, a body), an orange bar stops the arrow: it will slam into it. Status effects are the only words, and never with a number: they ride in the Execute button under the word Execute (the button doesn't repeat the move's name) (`statusText`: bleeds, can stun, slows them, sets them alight, crushing collisions, heals you, vents heat, you act sooner, harder while hot or low, takedowns vent and guard), so the bar never changes height. Cards out of a fight say more (`effectsText`), for building a moveset: the picture has the arrows, rings and dots, the card the words.
- **The left rail** (`CombatView.tsx`): the turn order (who acts now, then the next four) stacks down the left side above the heat bar, so nothing sits over the top of the board.
- **New shapes:** bolt (line of 4, stops at the first body; water doesn't block, cover does), pierce (line of 4, through every body), long shot (any line to the edge, not within 2), grapple (line of 2–3, first body), lob (2–4 away over anything, hits the tile and the four beside it), burst (all 8 around you).
- **Dash** puts you on the farthest open tile of the lane; **step-cut** steps you a tile straight back from the enemy hit (onto open, non-hazard deck). Neither earns a parting blow.

### Defensive moves
**Brace is the only defensive move every host starts with** (the plain, middling one): its guard now holds against every hit until your next turn (not just the next one), it still cools 25 and repairs 40% of structure, and it adds 10 Overload (`BRACE_OVERLOAD`). An ally's Aegis still soaks only the next hit. **Every other defensive move comes from an implant and is built from parts** (`DEF_HEADS`, `rollDefense`, `defendAction` in `augments.ts`; rules in `resolveDefend` in `fight.ts`). Helix arms and legs are always defensive; Crown's are 35% of the time, Patchwork's 25%, everyone else's 20%. Signature implants keep their heads.

Sizes are shares of max health or the structure bar, set so even a poor one gives back about one enemy hit (a defensive move costs your attack, and at threats 1 to 3 a hit takes 13 to 22% of a bare host's health).

| Move (slot) | Base (common) | Reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patch (arm) | repairs 35% of structure (allies: heals half that) | 2 |
| Cauterise (arm) | heals 22%, stops bleeding and burning, +12 heat | 2 |
| Mend (arm) | heals 20% | 3 |
| Counter (arm) | until your next turn, each hit may send a share back (you take the rest) | self |
| Anchor (leg) | physical hits 40% softer, can't be shoved or dragged | 1 |
| Fall Back (leg) | steps up to 2 tiles away from the nearest enemy, repairs 20% | self |
| Dig In (leg) | only if you haven't moved: repairs 25%, physical hits 20% softer | self |
| Second Wind (leg) | only under half health: steps 1 tile back, heals 28% | self |
| Sp. Guard (eyes) | special hits 40% softer and heat you half as much | 2 |

- **Rarity** scales amounts ×1 to ×2.1 (a legendary Surge Mend heals 42%) and guards +7% a step (cap 85%).
- **Coupling** decides what Dig In, Fall Back and Second Wind give back: hydraulic structure, arc or thermal health, servo both at 60%.
- **Core** is timing for heals and repairs: hot (Surge: all now, +6 heat), stock (60% now and 30% a turn for 2 turns: 120% in total), cold (Drip: nothing now, 50% a turn for 3 turns: 150% in total, −8 heat), heat pump (all now, +15 Overload). For guards: hot +10% and +6 heat, cold −5% and −8 heat, pump +15 Overload.
- **Lens** is reliability: clear always lands at 90%; cracked rolls 60% to 150%. For a counter it's chance against share: clear always triggers and sends back 25%, stock 50% for 40%, cracked 25% for 60%; rarity raises both (a legendary cracked counter: 45% for 96%).
- **Targeting** (moves with reach): Focused covers you or one ally, whoever you tap (by default the most hurt), +25%; Split covers you and one ally; Wide covers you and every ally in reach, −20%. Allies have no structure bar: repairs heal them at half strength. A counter never covers an ally.
- **Mods**: Purge (stops bleeding and burning), Coolant (−10 heat), Hastes you, Charge (+15 Overload), Rooted (can't be shoved). **Quirks** (epic and legendary): Last stand (twice as strong under 30% health), Jolting (a counter also stuns).
- Drips land at the start of your turns (`regen`); guards, counters and anchors last until your next turn (`ward`). Defensive implants grow the brace tree, with part upgrades of their own: Longer drip (cold core), Hair trigger (counter: more often), Clean burn (hot core or pump), Steady hand (clear lens), and two-part capstones Bulwark (clear lens + Anchor, Sp. Guard or Dig In), Lifeline (cold core + Mend, Patch or Cauterise), Riposte (cracked lens + Counter).
- **On the board** while one is picked: the bars of whoever it covers flash green on their empty part (bright for now, faint for what drips in later; repairs on a thin structure bar under yours); its guard outlines whoever it covers (amber against physical, violet special, white both, dashed for a counter or anchor alone); everyone you could tap to cover instead has a faint green dashed outline; a step shows the cyan arrow and a dashed cyan outline where you'll land. The Execute button names only the effect, never a number (counters, stuns, can't be shoved, stops bleeding and burning, charges Overload, you act sooner); the numbers are on the cards, for building between fights. Its button shows a short headline ("heal 20%", "35% counter", "guard 47%"); Dig In and Second Wind grey out when they can't be used. The balance bot keeps one heal or repair on its bar when an implant gives one and uses it under 45% health; `HEALER=1 npm run sim` gives every host a rare Crown Mend arm to compare (it rests at servers far less, with the same win rates and fight lengths).

### Upgrades
- **Every implant grows its own tree** (`content/upgrades.json`, `treeFor` in `engine/upgrades.ts`, owned nodes in `useAugmentTrees`), worked out from the part itself, so the same part always grows the same tree and two parts with one name grow different ones. The tree's own dice come from a hash of the implant's id, never the run's `random()`. Attack implants draw from the attack kinds, Helix braces from the brace kinds, the rest from the passive kinds. **Each body slot has its own upgrades** (`slots` on a kind; six times as likely as others, and never on another slot), and most change how you play through perks (`perk`, `TreePerk`, `withTreePerks`) rather than adding stats: brain Cold logic (sheds 3 more heat a round), Focus (+15% to enemies under 30% health) and the capstone Clear head (act twice at 30 heat or less); eyes Flank reader, First look (first hit each fight always full damage), Hunter's eye; ears Sound map (parting blows hurt 30% less), Inner ear, Echo sense (the first heavy aimed at you misses); nose Filters (hazards hurt half), Blood scent, Sealed (hazards can't hurt you); lungs Deep breath (bracing vents more), Second wind (bracing heals 4%), Iron lungs; spine Load path (structure +15%), Shock spine (take 5% less), Keel (once per host, survive a killing blow); legs Sprint start (+1 movement point), Footwork, Step dance; arms Finisher, Grip, Closer.
- **Parts grow their own upgrades** (`parts` on a kind, `partsOf` and `partsFit` in `upgrades.ts`): some upgrades only turn up on implants with a matching part, and when they can, four times as often. Lens: Ground lens (clear: +12% damage, −1 heat), Rezeroed (cracked: +8%, steadier). Coupling: Arc feed (arc or thermal: +10%, pushes 3 more heat, a little more special), Hard line (hydraulic or servo: +2 attack, a little more physical, crushes). Core: Chilled core (cold: +10%, −3 heat), Stoked core (hot: +15%, +2 heat), Bellows (heat pump: pushes 4 more heat). Head: Widened head (a shape that hits several: +12%), Drive head (one that moves anyone: crushes). Mods: Serrated (bleed: +10%, slows), Jolt coil (stun: +15% stun), Siphon (drain: +12% drain), Flush line (coolant: vents 6 more). Passives: Rivets (plated housing), Featherweight (light), Gasket (sealed), Radiator (heat sink: sheds 2 more), Damper (baffle: special hits heat you 25% less), Kindling (primer: +3 attack).
- **Synergy capstones** need two parts together and are eight times as likely when they fit: Thermite (arc or thermal coupling + hot core or heat pump: sets them alight, pushes heat), Cold sight (clear lens + cold core: always full damage, vents 10), Battering ram (hydraulic coupling + a head that moves them: knocks back and crushes), Scattershot (cracked lens + a wide head: +35%, slows), Bloodline (bleed and drain mods: bleeds, drains 30%). Passives: Heat plate (plated + heat sink), Faraday cage (sealed + baffle: special hits heat you half as much), Ghost frame (light + Quick), Furnace (primer + Charged or Tough). On the tree they ring violet and their card says "Paired capstone" (`paired` on the node); legendary capstones ring gold. Upgrades can now push heat (`transfer`) and shift the damage mix (`phys`). The tree is cached by its parts too, so two implants never share a tree by name alone.
- **Rarity sets the shape** (`shapes`): common 5 nodes, 3 deep, at most 2 branch ends; uncommon 7, 3 deep, 3 ends; rare 9, 4 deep, 4 ends; epic 11, 4 deep, 4 ends (the root forks three ways); legendary 13, 5 deep, 5 ends (three-way root). The root always forks; after that a branch grows longer three times as often as it forks again, so trees come out lopsided. Numbers grow with rarity (×1 common to ×1.45 legendary).
- **The maker sets what turns up:** each kind lists the makers that lean on it, and a maker's own kinds are three times as likely (Volkov servo boosts, rams and overdrive; Kizuna gyro sights and target locks; CBN barbs and leech lines; IOA jammers and snare code; Crown leeching and coolant; PARSU crush plates; Tianxia rams and coolant; Patchwork anything). A kind never repeats down one branch and rarely repeats in the tree.
- **Capstones** end every branch; deeper ones are stronger (×0.6 near the root up to ×1 at full depth), and they don't repeat in a tree while there are others to pick. **Only a legendary implant** ends its deepest branch in a **legendary capstone**: Wildfire (sets them alight), Redline (+50% damage while hot), Last stand (double damage while low) or Reaper (a takedown vents heat and guards you) on attack implants, Continuity on braces, Apex on the rest.
- **Costs** by depth: 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3. A node needs its parent; taking one child of a node closes its other children and everything under them.
- Stored per implant (by id): a part keeps its upgrades if you take it out. Movement from trees doesn't stack across implants (the best counts).
- **Points:** one for every fight won (`winsPerPoint`), shown as "next in N wins". Selling an implant, giving it to an ally or giving it up at an event gives back every point sunk into it. Trees and points start fresh with each host, like the implants.
- **The screen** draws the tree with the price on each node; tapping one shows its name, one line of what it does, and a Take button.
- The implants screen is a body map: each slot is marked on a host figure with a card per slot down each side.

## 5. Progression and loot

**Where:** `client/src/lib/combat/loot.ts`, `client/src/lib/combat/skills.ts`, `client/src/lib/combat/actions.ts` (enemy scaling).

### Enemy scaling by threat
- Base health: 36 + 17 × threat + 0.85 × threat². Attack +4.4 and defence +2.4 per threat on top of each enemy's base. Move damage +19% per threat.
- On top of that, every enemy's health is × 0.39 × 1.06^(threat − 1) and its attack × 1.34 × 1.047^(threat − 1) (`ENEMY_TUNING`, `ENEMY_GROWTH`). Fights stay short early, and the compounding growth means each host meets a wall it can't push through yet.
- Enemies alone are then eased (`ENEMY_EASE`): × 0.9 health and × 0.82 attack, and their attack grows × 1.03 a threat instead of × 1.047. Allies are built from the uneased numbers.
- Hosts are meant to grow strong: by the time a signal has lost a few hosts (firmware levels, tree picks, allies), the early and middle rooms should feel easy. The difficulty lives at the first-life fight and in the deep rooms.
- **Levels come in sectors of 10.** The 5th level of each sector is an **elite** (×1.3 health, ×0.95 attack; Cartel Enforcer, CBN Extraction Surgeon, Unregistered Operator in turn). The 10th is a **boss** (×1.15 health, ×0.95 attack): **The Fitter** at threat 20 (the Volkov foundry: Armed, a whole-row Press Line heavy, and at half health it bolts on a leg and becomes Fast) and **The Listener** at 30 (the drowned spire: Sighted, Focused and Alert, an Undertow ring heavy, and at half health it becomes Enduring). Bosses carry innate traits on top of their implants and turn once below half health (`traits` and `enrage` in `enemies.json`). Regular rooms name their enemy on the map (`enemy` in the map file), so each place has its own people; anything not named walks the regular rotation.
- Threat 10 is the **Patchwork King**, built from a level-10 frame with his own ×1.5 health, ×1.3 attack, ×1.25 defence and three scripted phases.
- Enemies carry 1–3 random common or uncommon implants.
- **First life only:** every threat-3 enemy is a **set piece** (×1.8 health, ×2.1 attack), whichever threat-3 room it waits in and whatever that room names it, so the first life can't route around it. It ends most first runs (the bot dies at threat 2 or 3 in about 90% of first lives).
- **Early threats bite** (`EARLY_BITE`): enemy attack ×1.5 at threat 1 for the first life (×1.45 for the hosts after it, who still run without firmware until the first boss falls), easing in a straight line to ×1 by threat 6, so a fresh host feels every hit.
- **Enemies past the first boss are built for firmware** (`FIRMWARE_ERA`): from chapter one's threat 6, and in every later chapter, enemies have ×1.35 health and ×1.4 attack. A host with firmware meets them on even terms; a host without it (the challenge run) is outmatched.
- **The Patchwork King** is the set-piece wall (`KING_MULT`) and more (`KING_EXTRA`: ×1.6 health, ×1.4 attack): a host with fresh firmware takes a few tries.
- **The middle rooms are real fights for a young signal** (`MID_BITE`): threats 6 to 9 hit 10% harder (and have 70% of the health), easing back to normal by threat 15. A seasoned signal outgrows them (play-i tree, firmware levels, allies). The curve the simulator holds (chapter one, threats 4–9): about 92% won with 1–2 hosts lost (check: 75–95%), 95% or more with 5+ (check: 93%+).
- Simulator targets: about 7 turns for a regular fight, about 14 for a boss. Median best level is about 9 after 5 hosts lost and 18–20 after 10.

### Drops: strip them for parts
- **Implants only come off bodies.** Every enemy carries real implants (`carried`, rolled by `rollCarried` in `loot.ts` when it's created): 1 up to threat 5, 2 up to 10, then 3, one per body slot, each built from parts (see Implant parts). It's worn gear, so it rolls at the drop odds of **six threats lower** (`CARRIED_QUALITY_LAG`): at threat 1–2 odds that's commons and a few uncommons; rares appear from threat 3 odds, epics from 5, legendaries from 6, and commons stop around 20. Half are used gear: 35% worn, 15% degraded.
- Their stats count for less on an enemy as quality rises (common and uncommon ×1, rare ×0.6, epic ×0.4, legendary ×0.25), so a lucky epic doesn't make an early enemy unkillable. The traits they give still come from the body slot.
- **Signature implants:** each boss and elite carries one (`signature` in `enemies.json`, the item in `items.json` with `signature` and its own `heads`): Cartel Enforcer *Collector's Hook*, CBN Surgeon *Surgeon's Hands*, Unregistered Operator *Unlogged Legs*, the Fitter *Fitter's Crane Arm*, the Listener *Listener's Array*, the Patchwork King *Patchwork Crown*. The name and slot are fixed; quality rolls every time (bosses epic, or legendary 15% + 1% per threat up to 50%; the King's crown legendary 35%; elites uncommon or better, rare or better from threat 15) and so do the head (from its own list, its signature head only at legendary) and mods. Signature implants never appear in shops or random rolls.
- **After a win you strip the bodies:** the win screen opens with a highlighted card listing every beaten enemy's implants by body; each part has a round "+" button that pulses until you choose; tapping a part selects it, and the gold button below reads "Take" and the part's name (two picks with Pick of the wreck; dots by the "Salvage" title show how many are left). Leaving stays locked until you pick; a small "Choose for me" link keeps the rarest. The rest are scrapped for a quarter of their sell price (at least 2 credits each).
- First life: the first enemy carries a common arm (an active move) and the second an uncommon passive, so both kinds are met early.
- Stims from threat 2: 15% chance rising to 25%. Bosses always drop one.
- Credits: 10 + 8 × threat, plus up to the same again at random. The King pays 160–220 and a stim.

### XP
Level n needs 100 × 1.5^(n−1) XP. Threats 1–5 give 120 / 150 / 210 / 230 / 250 XP, then +20 per threat.

## 6. Economy

**Where:** `client/src/lib/combat/economy.ts`, `client/src/lib/stores/useMarket.tsx`.

- Buy prices: common 45, uncommon 95, rare 190, epic 360, legendary 640 credits, scaled by condition (worn ×0.75, degraded ×0.5, corroded ×0.3).
- Selling returns 40% of the buy price (at least 5).
- The Grey Lane stocks 7 implants and 3 stims, rerolled after every fight. Overclock appears from threat 5. From threat 12 there is a 25% chance (rising 3% a threat, up to 60%) of one legendary.
- **Guns** (`client/src/lib/combat/guns.ts`): sold only in the Grey Lane, built from parts like implants. A gun isn't an implant, so **it never heats you**; instead it holds a few rounds a fight (`gunAmmo`, `gunMag` on the fight state), full at the start of each fight, and **any turn you don't attack reloads it** (Brace, a defensive move, or passing: `reloadGun`). An empty gun greys out ("Empty") until then; its button shows rounds left as pips.
  - **Parts:** frame (maker and model line: one per maker and action in `content/weapons.json`, just a name and a description of the object; `weapons2059.ts` turns them into frames), **action** (pistol: a bolt, 3 rounds, ×1.6 an implant attack of the same rarity; revolver: pierces a line, 2 rounds, ×1.85; shotgun: a cone, 2 rounds, ×1.5 on each target; base 12, `GUN_BASE`), **barrel** (the lenses: clear always full, cracked rolls low but hits 12% harder), **round** (slug 90% physical; flechette 60% physical and bleeds; thermal 20% physical and pushes 4 heat into them; EMP 10% physical and 15% stun), **mods** (the maker's extra on uncommon and better: Volkov knockback, Tianxia vents 6 of your heat, CBN bleed, IOA 15% stun; an extended magazine, +1 round, on epic and legendary). Makers lean: Volkov clear barrel and slugs, Tianxia cracked and slugs or flechette, CBN flechette, IOA EMP or thermal. Rarity scales damage like an implant's (×1 to ×2.1) and price (¤160 to ¤950; sells for 40%).
  - **Names:** the maker's model line with the action's letter ("VK-P5 Operator Sidearm"; its move "VK-P5 Pistol").
  - **The rack** (`generateGunRack`, `ensureGuns`, `buyGun` in `useMarket`): three guns at the stall's grades, restocked **only for a new host or a server rest** (`rests` in the map state), so fighting can't cycle it; stocking it leaves the fights' dice alone. Buying puts it in your hand. Implants and stims still restock after every fight.
  - **Trees:** a carried gun grows an upgrade tree on the Upgrades page (the attack kinds, none of a body slot's; `takeGun`), with gun upgrades of its own: Extended mag (+1 round), Hot loads (thermal rounds), the capstone Drum magazine (+2 rounds); barrels and mods grow the lens and mod upgrades. Selling a gun gives its points back.
  - Save version 16 sold the old fixed guns back at what they cost.
  - No screen uses blur or glow blobs: sheets and dialogs dim the screen with a solid shade, and the title, Grey Lane, victory and death screens have no blurred glows.
  - **Balance** (sim, 20 runs, against `NOGUNS=1`): guns take about 30% of attacking turns; win rates hold; The Fitter takes about 1 turn longer, the last boss about 6 shorter, and more runs beat the chapter (10 of 20 against 6), so guns help a little rather than not at all.

## 7. Death and the signal

**Where:** `rebirth` in `client/src/lib/stores/useCombat.tsx`.

- When the host dies, the signal jumps to a new body at threat 1, in the chapter's entry or at a beaten boss's server (see Servers and beaten bosses). Implants, credits, weapons, loadout extras and upgrades are lost. The host count, the firmware opened, the play-i tree and allies are kept.
- **Firmware opens from bosses** (`opens` and `holder` in `content/firmware.json`; `firmwareHeldAt`, `holderOf`, `openingXp` in `engine/firmware.ts`; `opened`, `fresh` in `useFirmware`). Every host is Unclassified until one beats chapter one's threat-5 boss, which holds the **Enforcer**; the Patchwork King holds the **Operative**, so chapter one ends with two. Each later chapter's threat-5 boss holds one more: the Deltas the **Courier**, Helix Hills the **Foreman**, the Rain Machine the **Wretch**, the Corridor the **Splice**. Nothing says so when the boss falls: the next host's death screen offers it, tagged New (`fresh`), and firmware is only ever loaded when a host wakes. A firmware opens at its own `start` level (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 in that order) or the best level any firmware has reached, whichever is higher, so it's usable the moment you get it. Once any is open, "no firmware" (Unclassified) stays on the list last: past the first boss it's a challenge run.
- **Each holder fights with its firmware** (`withFirmware` in `actions.ts`, used by `planFoes`): always the same enemy (an elite on threat 5, called back too), with the firmware's traits and moves. Chapter one's threat 5: a Volkov Operator with Hydraulic Slam (a line of 2 that knocks back), a telegraphed Piledriver (the ring round it) and Pain Suppression (armed, braced). The King keeps his phases, now shots down lines (bolt, pierce, beam; the heavy blast stays), sighted and focused. The Deltas: an IOA Interdiction Officer with Flicker Jab, Limiter Off and Blink (fast, alert). Helix Hills: a Fan Technician with Crowd Press, Clear the Floor and Overrun, all heat (enduring, braced). The Rain Machine: a Valley Raider fighting with its own implants' moves and an overdriven heavy (armed, enduring). The Corridor: an Unlogged Technician with Graft Whip, Drink and Resonance (enduring, alert).
- **Firmware is the host's class** (`identity` in `content/firmware.json`; `withClass`, `identityOf` in `engine/firmware.ts`; perks `chain`, `reflexMove`, `doubleActionHeat`, `extraCooling`, `heatGenBonus`, `ventMult`, `transferBonus`, `surgeKind`). Each firmware has its own rule, its own heat style and its own Overload ultimate, shown on the Self page and the firmware choice after a death:
  - **Stat lines:** see Damage (each class has health, structure, Attack, Defence, Sp. Atk, Sp. Def and Speed). Implants, trees and forks build on top, so any class can be pushed toward tougher or harder-hitting.
  - **Forks** (`choose` in a firmware's levels; `levelOptions`, `stepsTo`, `setForkChoices`; `forks` in `useFirmware`): levels 4, 7 and 10 offer two sides. They're chosen on the death screen, under the firmware picked for the next host, and hold for that host's whole life (the Self page shows them; respecs are for later). A fork can change the attack (shape, damage, effects), add speed, add a class perk (`perk`) or add max health (`hp`). Enforcer: Breaker (crush) or Vent engine (bursts ×1.5); Stun lock (25% stun) or Bulk (+30 health); Piledriver (hits far harder, 35% stun) or Earthshaker (hits all eight tiles round you). Operative: Optical lock (always full damage) or Run and gun (step back after the shot); Through the line (pierce) or Long eye (a shot that wants 4+ tiles); Clean line (far harder, through cover) or Deep cold (act twice up to 40 heat, sheds 2 more). Courier: Hit and run or Vault; Quicker (+2 speed) or Bleeder; Overclocked (act twice every turn) or Blur (+1 speed, far harder). Foreman: Clear the floor (all eight tiles) or Long shove (a shoving line of three); Stoker (+6 heat pushed) or Crusher (crush, 20% stun); Overrun (3x3 blast) or Pressure (+10 heat pushed, harder).
  - **Enforcer** (melee, Volkov): a takedown gives another action. Runs hot fast (+3 heat on every hit) and its venting bursts hit 50% harder. Ultimate **Piledriver**: twice your best move on everyone within 2 tiles, knocking them back.
  - **Operative** (ranged, Kizuna): acts twice in a turn started at 25 heat or less. Runs cold (sheds 3 more heat a turn). Ultimate **Overwatch**: a precise shot (1.2× your best move) at every enemy within 6 tiles.
  - **Courier** (swift, IOA): a free step after every attack made without moving first, plus its speed. Runs warm and keeps moving. Ultimate **Blink**: no hit; all your heat vented, hasted for 2 rounds, and you act again at once.
  - **Foreman** (crowd, Tianxia): every hit pushes 6 more heat into whoever it lands on (cooking crowds toward overheating). Ultimate **Overrun**: 0.8× your best move on everyone within 3 tiles, each shoved back and pumped with 50 heat.
  - **Splice** (late; Patchwork; held by the Corridor's first boss): **each passive slot (brain, ears, nose, lungs, spine) can hold a second, compatible implant** (`spliced` in `useInventory`; `canSplice`, `SPLICE_KIN` in `augments.ts`): the same maker always takes, and so does Patchwork with anyone; otherwise Helix–Crown, Helix–PARSU, Crown–Kizuna, Crown–Tianxia, PARSU–Volkov, PARSU–Tianxia, Volkov–IOA, IOA–Kizuna, IOA–CBN, CBN–Patchwork. Both implants' stats count, and both grow their upgrade trees from the one pool of points (the Upgrades page shows a spliced one with a "+"). Up to 2 pairs to start (`splicePairs`). Each pair costs heat every turn (+2, `SPLICE.heatPerPair`, cut by `spliceHeatCut`) and 10% of the structure bar (`withSplice`). Taking the fitted implant out, or swapping it for one its partner doesn't take to, brings the partner out too; loading any other firmware takes every spliced part back out. Stats 95 / 12 / 5: the weakest base, built from what it wears. Attack **Graft Whip**: a reach of 2 that heals 10% of the damage. Ultimate **Resonance**: 0.6× your best move for every spliced pair worn (at least one, up to three) on everyone within 3 tiles. Forks: Tolerance (each pair 1 less heat) or Overgrowth (a third pair); Graft hunger (heals 25%, harder) or Thorns (+25 health); Chimera (two more pairs) or Symbiote (pairs cost no heat). Spliced pairs show on the Implants page under the slot ("Spliced beside it", with "Take out"), and a spare that fits shows a gold "Splice" button.
  - **Wretch** (late; held by the Rain Machine's first boss): 80 / 10 / 3, the worst stats, no rule, no special attack (Strike) and no special ultimate (the plain Signal Surge). It takes to implants: implant moves hit 35% harder (`implantDamage`, `fromImplant` on implant moves), active implants' upgrade nodes cost a point less (never below 1; `setTreeDiscount`), a fifth move slot, but implants' health costs hit it 50% harder. Its heat is whatever its implants' makers make it. Levels add 5% implant damage; forks: Tolerance (health costs back to normal) or Overdrive grafts (+15%); Sixth slot or Cool grafts (implant moves 3 heat cheaper); Kinship (implant mods count twice: stun, drain and coolant doubled) or Abomination (active implants give 50% more stats). Splice is the passive-implant build; the Wretch is the active one.
  - **Unclassified** is every host's state until the first boss falls, and can always be picked on the death screen after that (the challenge run): Strike, the plain surge, every upgrade tree a point cheaper, and none of the firmware lift.
- **Firmware is fixed for a host's whole life:** it's chosen only on the death screen, for the next host. During a run you can change allies, implants and your moves, never the firmware.
- **Firmware replaces Strike and grows over many hosts** (`content/firmware.json`, `engine/firmware.ts`, `useFirmware`): with firmware loaded, its attack takes Strike's place on the Moves screen and the fight bar. Every room fight won with it loaded gives that firmware xp (1, an elite 3, a boss or the set piece 6), kept per firmware across hosts; levels 1 to 10 take 4, 6, 9, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28 and 32 xp. Its damage is a scale times the wearer's physical attack, and each level adds one thing. **Hydraulic Slam** (Enforcer): a line of 2, ×1.15, then harder hits, knockback (level 3), crush (5), a 20% stun (7), cooler (9) and Piledriver at 10 (35% stun). **Precision Shot** (Operative): a bolt down a line of 4, ×1.0, then harder hits, cooler (3), always full damage (5), through every body in the line (7), cooler (9) and Clean line at 10 (through cover). The Self page shows the firmware's level, the xp to the next, its attack now and every level's step; a level gained shows on the victory screen. Unclassified hosts keep Strike.
- **The later firmware** (opened from bosses, as above; the death screen offers every one opened, `availableFirmware`). **Courier** (105 health, 15 attack, 4 defence, +2 speed): Flicker Jab, one tile next to you, ×0.95, +8 heat; it gains harder hits, +1 speed at levels 3 and 7, cooler, hit and run (5), then Afterimage at 9 (act twice in a turn while at 40 heat or less) and Overclocked at 10 (act twice every turn). Acting twice uses the double action (`perks.doubleActionHeat`, raised by `firmwarePassives`); the speed adds to `playerDrive`. **Foreman** (135 health, 15 attack, 7 defence): Crowd Press, the three tiles in front (sweep), ×0.85, +16 heat; knockback (3), all eight tiles around you (4), cooler, harder hits, a 20% stun (7), crush (9), and Overrun at 10, a 3x3 blast up to three tiles away.
- **Run numbers** (`engine/runStats.ts`, `useRunStats`): the Self page shows what this host has done, or every host together (a switch): fights won, the deepest threat, enemies, elites and bosses killed, damage dealt and taken, health healed, the biggest hit, critical hits, Signal Surges, the favourite move (most used, with its damage), allies' damage dealt and taken, stims used, parts taken and credits earned. They're counted from the fight state before and after each step (health lost, bodies dropped), credited to whoever acted (the player's move, an ally, an enemy, or nothing: hazards, burning). A new host starts its numbers from zero; the totals last until a save wipe.
- The Implants screen shows each implant's upgrade progress (a dot per level of its tree, gold where taken); the Upgrades screen shows an attack implant's shape on its chip and, above the tree, its attack as it stands with what's been taken.
- **The play-i tree** (`client/src/content/playi-tree.json`, `client/src/lib/engine/playi.ts`): what the signal keeps. 34 nodes in 6 branches, hidden from the player. After each death the player picks 1 of 2 random open nodes (a node is open when one of its required nodes is owned; tier 1 needs nothing; Network nodes need an unlocked ally). When fewer than two are open, a repeatable Signal fragment (+5 max health) fills the gap.
- **The tree is the signal, firmware is how a host fights.** No play-i node copies a firmware's rule. The heat branch serves every style: **Set point** (`heat_2a`: start every fight at 0 or at 50, chosen on the death screen when it's taken; `setPoint` in `usePlayi`), **Thermal tolerance** (less extra damage when hot), **Redline** (`heat_3`: once a fight, a turn started at 86+ heat gets a second action; `redlineUsed`). **Hit and fade** (`move_2a`: an attack made while warm, 36 to 60 heat, lets you step a tile after, moved or not). **Momentum** (`combat_5`: each takedown adds 20 to Overload). **Firmware resonance** (`combat_3b`, tier 3; `withResonance` in `firmware.ts`): the loaded firmware's own rule grows, each its own way: Enforcer chain kills also vent 15 heat; Operative acts twice up to 35 heat; Courier's step after attacking reaches 2 tiles; Foreman's overheating enemies spill 20 heat onto everyone beside them (which can set them off in turn); Splice gets one more pair; the Wretch's implant moves hit 10% harder; Unclassified's hot Strike sets them alight.
- Node effects reach the fight engine through `perks` on the fight state (cooling, double-action heat, brace repair and venting, damage, flanking, clean first strike, finisher, chain, hot-damage taken, damage taken, Holdfast, Sidestep, hazards), the new host (starting implant, Bio-Stims, upgrade points, max health, structure, health costs), movement (extra tiles, Reflex loop, Phase step, Runaway), the fight bar (Fifth slot), the market (Haggler) and loot (Pick of the wreck).
- Holdfast is once per host; Sidestep and Clean first strike reset every fight.

### Allies

**Where:** `client/src/content/allies.json` (roster), `client/src/lib/combat/allies.ts` (bodies, moves, levels), `client/src/lib/stores/useAllies.tsx` (unlocks, levels, party), the Allies part of `client/src/lib/engine/fight.ts`.

- Five allies, each met in a room of the map: **Bale** (Tianxia tank, the cartel weigh-station at threat 5, so the first ally comes early), **Matron** (Crown healer, the Deltas' raft-houses), **Ghostline** (Kizuna spotter, Helix Hills' square), **Ward** (Helix shield, the Rain Machine's last terrace), **Breaker** (Volkov damage, the Corridor's last rack); chapter one ends at the King, so only Bale is met there. Once the room is clear the panel offers "Link with …"; linking keeps them for every later host and they join now if there's room. Their stat level still comes from their old unlock level (10/15/20/25/30). Old saves (version 1) keep the allies they had unlocked.
- One ally per host (two with the Second link capstone), chosen on the death screen after the tree pick. The last host's ally is kept by default.
- **An ally's turn** (`startAllyTurn`; the store's `allyTurn`): the player plays it. The action bar shows the ally's moves instead of the player's (tap one to see it on the board, then Execute: `allyPicked`, `pickAllyMove`). Its attack shows the same previews as yours: enemy health bars flash, "Hit" floats over who it'll hit, shove arrows sit on their edges (`allyStruck`, `estimateAllyHit`); a defensive move flashes green on the bars of whoever it covers (`allyDefendTargets`). the ally's move tiles light up in its colour (tap one to move it; like the player's own move it can be changed, or undone, until the ally acts or its turn ends: `placeAlly` from `allyOrigin`), and using a move ends its turn; "End <name>'s turn" lets it wait. At the start of its turn its guard and draw-fire end, its cooldowns tick down and it burns; a stun or a climb out of the sea costs it the turn.
- **Each ally has a preset kit** (`kit` in allies.json; `kitItems`, `kitRarity` in `allies.ts`): two fixed implants built from parts like yours but never rolled, so every player's ally is the same. Its active implant is its attack (shape, damage type, side effects, targeting; the attack keeps the ally's own name), its passive gives stats and its structure bar. The kit's grade rises with the ally's level: common at 1–2, uncommon 3–4, rare 5–6, epic 7–9, legendary 10 (its attack grows with the grade instead of the old flat 8%; its body still toughens 8% a level). Kits: Bale TX arm (hydraulic, knocks back) and plated spine with a heat sink; Matron RX eyes (arc, clear lens, drains) and sealed lungs with a baffle; Ghostline KZ eyes (lance, arc, clear lens, through cover) and light ears with a baffle; Ward HX arm (servo, can stun) and sealed spine with a heat sink; Breaker VK arm (reach, hydraulic, hot core, crushes) and a plated brain with a primer; Tally IO arm (hook, Split 2, thermal, cracked lens) and a light brain with a primer; Ninety HX arm (grapple) and plated spine; Dust TX eyes (bolt, hydraulic) and a light nose with Quick. The content check wants real heads and mods.
- Each ally has its own two moves (its kit attack and its support move) and moves 2 tiles. Effects: **attack** (goes for your target if it can reach it, else the nearest enemy it can: `allyAimFoe`), **taunt** (every enemy within 3 tiles of the ally, `TAUNT_RANGE`, aims at it until its next turn; ally guards up), **heal** (a share of your max health, any range), **mark** (your hits on the target do more until your turn ends), **guard** (you guard up until the ally's next turn). Support moves have cooldowns of 2–3 of the ally's turns. What allies set up for you (a mark, Coordinated) is spent when your turn ends (`endPlayerTurn`).
- In your turn you can move the ally once (tap it, or its name, then a lit tile) and use one of its moves, before or after your own move. The turn ends when you execute.
- Enemies go for whoever is nearest, the player winning ties, unless an ally is drawing fire. A heavy wound up at an ally can be dodged by moving that ally.
- **Allies have a structure bar** from the implants they wear (their kit and yours, `structureOf`, × 1 + 0.1 per unlock level): physical hits wear it first, special goes to health, and your Patch repairs it. Allies never run hot: no heat, no Overload. A knocked-out ally sits out the rest of that fight only. Allies don't take hazard damage. Every fight starts them at full health.
- Stats: built like a regular enemy of the unlock level (health × the ally's `hp`, attack and defence from its base plus the level scaling), then × 0.55 health and × 0.75 attack (`ALLY_TUNING`), plus their implants. Move damage scales like an enemy move of the unlock level.
- Levels: +8% health, attack, defence and move damage a level, up to 10. Each win gives the party 1 XP (2 with Shared memory); level n to n + 1 takes n + 1 XP. Levels survive death, so an early ally can be raised for the late game.
- **Allies start beside or behind you** (`allySpawnPositions`): the nearest open tiles, never one closer to the enemies than you stand if another will do.
- **Stories:** each ally has three lines (`story` in allies.json) it lets slip at levels 3, 6 and 10 (`ALLY_STORY_AT`, `storySoFar`). They show on the Allies page under the link, and each goes into the journal when a level brings it out. They only build on the ally's blurb; the content check wants exactly three.
- Network perks: Handshake (+25% ally health), Shared memory (double ally XP), Coordinated (+20% damage on a foe your ally hit this round), Second link (two allies).
- **Implants on allies** (the Allies tile at the hideout, `screens/AlliesTab.tsx`): on top of its kit, you give each met ally **any one active** implant (`limb`) and **any one passive** (`other`). Giving one takes it out of the stash or off your body; whatever was in that slot goes back to the stash. Allies keep their implants when a host is lost, so they're the one place an implant survives death.
  - The active gives it a **third move**, whatever the implant does in your hands: an attack with its shape, damage type, targeting and side effects (knockback, drags, bleed, stun, slow, fire, crush, drain; moving yourself and venting are yours alone), damage scaled like the ally's own moves; or a defensive move, which covers the ally and, with reach, you and other allies within reach of it (the most hurt by default). The Allies page shows its own kit, the two slots (Active, Passive) and its moves.
  - Both add their stats (attack, defence, health, Sp. Atk and Sp. Def, passive parts' housing and traits) and their structure to the ally.
  - **Affinity** (`affinity` in allies.json): Bale Tianxia and PARSU, Matron Crown, Ghostline Kizuna and IOA, Ward Helix, Breaker Volkov and CBN. An affinity implant's stats count × 1.5 and a limb hits × 1.25 (`AFFINITY`).
- **Links**: every ally riding with the host gives you something, whatever it wears (`link` in allies.json, effect in `ALLY_LINKS`): Bale +10% structure bar, Matron every brace heals 5% of your health, Ghostline your first attack each fight lands in full, Ward parting blows do half damage, Breaker enemies you knock into things take +50% collision damage. The content check fails an ally without a link.

## 7b. The map

**Where:** `client/src/content/map-indian-ocean.json` (rooms), `client/src/lib/engine/map.ts` (rules), `client/src/lib/stores/useMap.tsx`, `screens/RoomPanel.tsx`.

- Chapter one is a web of 17 rooms, threats 1 to 10: it ends at the Patchwork King's throne. The hideout screen has a row of small pixel-icon buttons (Moves, Implants, Upgrades, Allies once met, Self), each opening a full page and showing an amber dot when something there waits (new salvage that fits, a path or point to spend, a free bar slot). Below it is the room you're in (about two sentences), what's there, and its exits as a list, each with a hint ("East: the smell of fuel"). Exits you've been through before carry a small dot.
- **Today's run** (`client/src/lib/daily.ts`): the title screen offers one run a day, the same for everyone: chapter one from a seed made from the UTC date (`dailySeed`), one life, no strain. The first life's set piece on threat 3 is skipped (`firstLifeAt`), so a single life doesn't end on a script. Death ends it: the deepest level won is the score, and the best each day is kept (`2059-daily`, outside the save; `recordDaily`). It runs in its own save slot (`2059-daily-save`, chosen by save.ts when the page loads while `2059-daily-on` is set), so the player's own game is never read or written; starting or leaving reloads the page. A run can be left from the title without counting.
- **Strain** (`client/src/lib/engine/strain.ts`, `useStrain`): finishing any chapter unlocks a Strain page in the hub with four switches: Thick skin (enemies +25% health), Hard hitters (+20% attack), Running hot (enemies start every fight 30 heat hotter, at most 95) and Shuttered (the Grey Lane is closed). Each switch on pays +25% credits per win, and two or more let you take one more part off the bodies. What you pick takes hold when the next host wakes (`chosen` and `active`), so it can't be dropped before a hard fight. With all four on the bot plays about as hard as before enemies were eased (`STRAIN=tough,fierce,hot,shut npm run sim`).
- **Room events** (`content/events.json`, placed by `event` on a fight room; `client/src/lib/engine/events.ts`; `chooseEvent` in the combat store): once a room's fight is won, someone there offers a choice, once per host. A choice can cost credits, max health for the rest of this host's life (the scar, `scar` in the map state, added to the implants' health cost) or your weakest part (carried before worn; signature parts never), can carry odds with a lose outcome, and can give an implant of a rarity (or one grade better than the part given), stims or credits. Outcomes are seeded per host, room and choice. The content check rejects bad entries, events outside fight rooms, events sharing a room with a market, ally or cache, and events placed nowhere. Chapter one's five: a pump-fleet fitter in the hull (10 max health for a rare implant), a blind boy in the lanes (your weakest part for one a grade better), casings thrown in the shacks (¤30, even odds of ¤80), a body in the net lofts (65% an uncommon implant, else the wired net bites for 6 max health), and an old woman with a broken-seal kit at the port (¤25 for two stims).
- **The last body** (`corpse` in the map state; `newHost`, `corpseHere`, `takeCorpse` in `map.ts`): when a host is lost, the strongest implant it wore (by power; a machine frame's built-in parts don't count) stays on its body in the room where it fell. Once that room's fight is won by a later host, the room card shows the body and a "Take it back" button that puts the part in the inventory. Only the latest body holds anything: losing another host before getting there loses the old part for good. Moving to a new chapter leaves it behind.
- **The signal's map** (`SignalMap.tsx`, `mapView` in `map.ts`) opens the room card: a small pixel map in 2.5D that draws only what any host has seen, with the room grid turned about 30 degrees (`TILT`) and seen from above at an angle (`SQUASH`), so north points up the screen and a little right and east points right and a little down. Each room walked into is a slab (its slanted top, and the two edges facing you); its height is how far up it sits (`roomHeights`: up exits climb a level, down exits drop one, other ways stay level; where loops of exits disagree, the heights that fit every exit best win, so a disagreement shows as a gentle slope). Each room floats at its height on a thin post, with its shadow on the ground beneath (no solid pillars, so nothing hides behind them); slabs are drawn back to front, halls run straight between slab tops. "+" and "−" buttons in the map's corner zoom in and out (2 to 5 screen pixels a map pixel); halls run from block top to block top as bridges, or ramps between levels. Rooms behind a door seen but not taken are dotted outlines at their height; the room you're in glows and holds a tiny host. Each room found shows one pixel symbol for what waits there, most pressing first (`roomMark`): a horned skull for a boss still standing, a skull for an elite, a small red blade mark for any other fight this host hasn't won, a figure in their colour for someone not yet linked, a crate for a cache not yet searched, a speech bubble for an event this host hasn't used, an eye for something not yet looked at, a server rack with a breathing light for a server, a gold coin for the Grey Lane, and a dim blade mark for a fight already won. Fights come back with each new host, so their marks do too. A glimpsed room behind a door that needs every goal shows a padlock. A dark stain spills over the corner of each room where a host was lost (`fell` in the map state). Where the last host fell, a gold glint flickers over the stain while its body still holds a part. It is kept across hosts, so each life fills in a little more; a new chapter starts a blank one. It follows the host, can be dragged to look around, and a tap makes it taller. Nothing else is shown: no names, no fights, no goals. A small needle and an N in the map's top-left corner show which way north is. In the room's "Ways on" list, each exit shows a pixel arrow pointing the way its hall runs on the map (`wayArrow`), so "North", "Up" or "In" can be matched to a hall at a glance. Each room's place on the map is `at: [x, y]` in the map file. `scripts/layout-maps.ts` lays the maps out (seeded): every way between two rooms is a one-step hall between neighbours, or a straight two-step hall with nothing in the middle; rooms that don't connect avoid sitting side by side, so they don't look joined; and the map stays compact. `--redo` lays every map out from scratch and then points north/south/east/west exits the way they're drawn (up, down, in and out keep their names); without it, it only places rooms that have no spot yet. The content check rejects two rooms on one spot. The fight button (or "Go on" once a chapter is done) sits at the bottom. There is no coaching card.
- **Threat comes from the room.** Fight rooms hold one threat level, rising with depth from the body dock (1) to the drowned spire (30). Elites sit on 5, 15 and 25, bosses on 10 (the Patchwork King), 20 and 30. The fight footer button starts the room's fight.
- **Enemies hold the room:** while a room's fight is uncleared, every exit but the one you came in by is blocked. Win and the room stays clear for that host.
- **Markets** are in three safe rooms (the body dock, the Grey Lane stall, the canopy stall). The Grey Lane is only reachable from those rooms.
- **Things to look at:** 21 rooms have something to look closer at once they're clear; what you see goes into the journal.
- **Health carries between fights** (`hurt` in the map state; `healthNow`, `hurtAfterWin`, `WIN_RECOVER` in `map.ts`): a host starts each room fight on the health it ended the last one with, plus 20% of its max health caught back after a win (never more than full, and never below 1). **There is no way out of a fight:** once it starts it ends in a win or the host's death (no retreat, flee or escape, ever). Resting at a server (a beaten boss's room) heals it fully; a new host starts whole. Practice fights start whole and don't count. The room screen shows the host's health under its name (red under 35%). The simulator's bot walks back through quiet rooms to rest at a server when it's under half health.
- **Fight bar and moves:** the turn button is a solid red "Execute" when an attack is chosen (blue for a brace) and a plain dark "Pass turn" otherwise. During the extra step after acting (Reflex loop) every move card is greyed out and tagged "Step only". On the Moves screen and the fight bar, an implant's move is coloured by the implant's rarity, and its card names the body slot it comes from; "Your guns" lists every gun you own with a "Carry" button (buying a gun carries it straight away).
- **Exits** read as what they are: an exit's own name if it has one ("A hatch, down"), otherwise what lies along it (its hint, "Tarp shacks, a dog barking"), with a small Up/Down tag where the way climbs or drops. No compass words; the arrow beside each shows which way it runs on the map.
- **Allies can be changed at a server** (who's with you, up to your ally slots), as well as after a host is lost.
- **Servers and beaten bosses** (`server` on a boss room in the map file: the text is what you see of it; `beaten`, `recalled`, `isServer`, `wakePlaces`, `rest`, `recall` in `map.ts`; `recallCost` in `economy.ts`): servers are rare and earned. Each later chapter's set piece (threat 10) and first boss (threat 20) sit in rooms with a pirate server; chapter one's is at the cartel weigh-station (threat 5), its first boss, since the chapter ends at the King. **A beaten boss stays down for every later host**, and its server wakes: the room shows a server on the map and becomes a place a new host can wake (the death screen offers the chapter's entry or any awake server), a place to **rest** (heal fully; every fight this host has won comes back, beaten bosses stay down, caches and events stay used; you can also change allies there) and a place to **call the boss back**: for ¤`recallCost` (about three fights' credits at its threat: 405 at threat 10, 765 at 20) it fights this host again at full strength, for its full drop. A call-back lasts for that host only, and winning it again changes nothing on the map. A boss's own signature implant (the Patchwork Crown, the Fitter's Crane Arm...) is marked with its boss's threat when it drops from a boss room, and sells for 90% of that boss's call-back cost (`BOSS_SELL_SHARE`), so farming a boss pays about what any fight pays, never more. Once beaten, a boss room reads its `quiet` text instead (the content check asks for one). The chapter's last boss (threat 30; chapter one's King on 10) ends the chapter and has no server. Markets are only markets. A fresh host waking at a deep server is still bare, next to rooms of that depth: going back to easier rooms (whose fights come back for every host) or saving credits is part of the choice. The content check keeps servers to threat-10 and threat-20 rooms.
- **Caches:** five rooms (pumps, generator, containers, smelt, drift) hide a cache that can be searched once per host once the room is clear: half the time a stim (never Overclock), otherwise 20–50 credits + 2 × threat. Seeded from the run seed and the room.
- **Upgrade points come from wins:** one per room fight won. A host can't refight a room it cleared, so points can't be farmed.
- **Hidden goals:** three (in chapter one the slab, the manifest and the ledger: placeholders until the story is written), each found by looking at something in a particular room. They're never listed. When all three are found, the sealed way up the steps to the patchwork throne opens.
- **Chapter ending:** winning the end room (`end` in the map file, the drowned spire) finishes the chapter. The victory screen shows the ending text, hosts used, places seen, things looked at and allies linked, and what the signal keeps. From then on the spire reads its `quiet` text and holds no fight for any host; the rest of the map keeps refilling, so the run can go on.
- **Death:** a new host is another corpse reanimating at the body dock. Fights and caches refill; visited rooms, things looked at, allies met and goals are kept.
- The content check rejects exits to missing rooms, unreachable rooms, a goal in more or fewer than one room, and fights in market rooms. It also rejects an ally waiting in more or fewer than one room, and a named enemy that doesn't exist or sits outside a regular room. The balance simulator walks the map like a player: it takes the shortest way through cleared rooms to the easiest uncleared fight, looks at things, links allies and searches caches on the way.

### Tides

**Where:** the Tides part of `client/src/lib/engine/fight.ts` (`TIDE`, `startTide`, `tideTick`).

- Drawn arenas can mark mud flats with `m`. A board with mud has a tide; one without has none.
- Fights start at low tide. At the start of every round the tide counts down, and every 3 rounds (`TIDE.every`) it turns. When it comes in, the mud becomes open sea: anyone standing on it is in the water, with no damage, can't be hit, and spends their next turn climbing out onto the nearest dry ground (enemies also lose a wound-up heavy). When it goes out, the mud is ground again.
- Enemies keep off mud the tide takes next round: they won't stop on it, and an approach walk stops short of it.
- The HUD shows the tide and when it turns ("Tide comes in next round · get off the mud"), and mud about to go under glows blue. The board keeps its size while the mud is under water.

### The alarm and dreamers (chapter rules)

**Where:** `rules` on a chapter in `chapters.json`; the Alarm and Dreamers parts of `client/src/lib/engine/fight.ts`.

- **Alarm** (`rules.alarm`): every fight starts quiet. At the start of round 3 the alarm trips: lights and air go off. From the next round, everyone's heat rises by 4 at the start of every round (`ALARM.heat`), and anyone beside a cold pod (`o` in an arena: cover that glows blue) sheds 12 instead (`ALARM.cold`). The floor goes dark (never red: red means danger); tiles beside a pod stay blue. A fight drawn in another chapter's look (a dream) has no alarm. The HUD counts down to the alarm and then says whether you're cool.
- **Dreamers** (`rules.flinch`): hosts still have a sleeper inside. At the start of your turn, once a fight at most, there's a 1 in 5 chance (`FLINCH.chance`) they flinch: you take one step you didn't choose, onto open empty ground beside you (never a hazard, water or a body). Only chapters with dreamers roll for it, so other chapters' seeded runs are unchanged.

### Wind (chapter rule)

**Where:** `rules.wind` on a chapter; the Wind part of `client/src/lib/engine/fight.ts` (`WIND`, `startWind`, `windTick`, `dangerTiles`).

- The wind blows one way (rolled from the fight's seed) and pushes everyone one tile that way as each of your turns ends, downwind bodies first so a line moves together. Cover, the edge of the board and other bodies stop a body (no damage); over a drop it goes in, like a knockback into the sea; onto a hazard, it stands in it. Every 3 of your turns (`WIND.every`) it turns to a new direction.
- **The red zone is honest about it** (`dangerTiles`): enemies are judged from where the wind leaves them, and a tile is red if the push would carry you from it into their reach (over an edge isn't red: in the water you can't be hit). The bot reads the same zone. Without wind it's the plain union of each enemy's reach.
- The HUD shows the direction and when it turns; faint streaks drift across the floor with it.

### Orders and machine hosts (the finale's rules)

**Where:** `rules.orders` and `rules.machines` on a chapter; the Orders part of `client/src/lib/engine/fight.ts` (`ORDERS`, `ORDER_TEXT`, `ordersTick`, `rewriteOrder`); `MACHINE_FRAMES` in the combat store.

- **Orders:** every fight starts with an order in force (seeded), and a new one is issued every 2 rounds. Orders bind everyone: *heavy attacks are refused* (no one winds up a heavy), *hold position* (nobody moves: you, enemies, allies), *every strike is logged in full* (every hit lands in full), *thermal reset* (everyone vents to zero heat as it comes in), *nothing leaves its tile* (no knockbacks, drags or pulls). Once a fight you can rewrite the order in force to any of them; it then holds for a full term. A bar under the HUD shows the order, what it does and how long it holds.
- **Machine hosts:** in a chapter of machines, every new host wakes as one of three frames (seeded): a loader (two legs and a chassis), a gantry (two arms and a rail) or a drone swarm (optics, core and pickups), their parts fitted (legendary signature items in items.json, never dropped or sold). The frames stand in for the allies this chapter doesn't have, which is why they're legendary.

## 7c. Chapters

**Where:** `client/src/content/chapters.json` (order, map, cast, tileset, ready), `client/src/lib/combat/chapters.ts`, `MAPS` in `client/src/lib/engine/map.ts`.

- Chapters are played in the order of `chapters.json`. Each names its map (`content/map-<map>.json`, registered in `MAPS`), its **cast** and its tileset. The map store records which chapter the signal is in (`chapter`); everything that depends on the place reads `activeChapter()`.
- **Cast:** regular enemies walk the `rotation` across the regular threat levels; `elites` guard threat 5, 15 and 25 in turn (a chapter's threat 5 is its firmware holder, see Firmware); `bosses` hold 20 and 30 (and every 10 after, in turn); `firstFight` is the first host's set piece on threat 3; `setPiece` is the chapter's built fight on 10 (chapter one: the Patchwork King). Enemies start at their own `startingHeat` (bosses used to start at 50 whatever their data said).
- **Moving on:** once a chapter's end fight is won, and only if the next chapter is marked `ready`, the victory screen offers "Go on" (or "Stay here a while"; the hideout's bottom button then offers it). Going on wakes a new host at the next chapter's entry with a fresh map: implants, credits, weapons, loadout and upgrades stay behind as on a death, while allies, their implants, the play-i tree and the journal carry on. It counts as a new host but offers no play-i card. A chapter can't be returned to.
- **Tilesets:** arena characters pick a slot in the chapter's tileset (`#` `d` `n` `p` = cover 1–4, `f` `c` `b` `h` = hazards 1–4), so each chapter draws its own. Burning hazards (`fire`, `oil`) set whoever ends a turn on them alight. Chapter two draws burning oil, silt sumps, record cases and mooring bollards.
- **Chapter two, the Drowned Deltas** (`map-deltas.json`, 32 rooms, threats 1–30, most arenas with mud flats). Cast: SALV Boarder, Silt Diver, Hull-Bound Deckhand, Checkpoint Scanner, Record Courier, IOA Interdiction Officer, the Recurring and the CBN surgeon in rotation; elites the Silt Matriarch (5), Checkpoint Commander (15) and Unregistered Operator (25); **the Echo** on 10; bosses the Living Vessel (20) and the Registrar (30, the end room, sealed until the stamp, the mirror and the case are found). New ally: **Tally** (met in the queue; IOA/Kizuna affinity; link: the first heavy aimed at you each fight misses). Signatures: Commander's Stamp, Living Keel, Registrar's Lens.
- **The Echo** (`createEcho`): wears up to three of the implants your last body wore when the signal left it (death or going on; best first, `usePlayi.lastBody`), with their attacks as its moves, the strongest also as a heavy (×1.6, "held too long"), and the King's multipliers. Stripping it gets one of your old implants back. With nothing remembered it wears what it found.
- **Chapter three, Helix Hills** (`map-helix-hills.json`, 33 rooms in three tiers: the wards, the staff town, the Ridge; rules: alarm and dreamers). Cast: Continuation Officer, Sleepwalker, Night Orderly, Fan Technician, Lattice Thief, Academy Graduate, the Helix Warden and the Kizuna Auditor in rotation; elites the Ward Sister (5), Academy Prefect (15) and Unregistered Operator (25); **the Long Sleeper** on 10 (`createSetPiece`: a King-sized wall whose moves are remembered chapter-one fights, fought in the deep ward, which is chapter one's throne room mirrored and misremembered, drawn in chapter one's tileset via the room's `tileset`); bosses the Count (20) and the First Sleeper (30, the end room, sealed until the count sheet, the file and the photo are found). Tileset: steam vents, live cables, coolant spills and open grates; staff lockers, gas canisters, cot stacks and ward terminals. New ally: **Ninety** (met in ward seven; Helix/Crown affinity; link: your host's sleeper never flinches). Signatures: Sister's Keys, Sleeper's Eyes, the Count's Hand, Lattice Heart.
- **Chapter four, the Rain Machine** (`map-rain-machine.json`, 28 rooms: the dry valley, the terraces and dam, the towers and summit; every way climbs; rule: wind; `~` here is a drop). Cast: Terrace Worker, Seeding Drone, Valley Raider, Air-Lift Rigger, Dam Keeper, Chamber Technician, the Tianxia Compliance Unit and the Ghost-MIL Infiltrator in rotation; elites the Shift Foreman (5), Ghost-MIL Captain (15) and Drone Shepherd (25); **the Overseer** on 10; bosses the Storm Tower (20) and the Weather (30, the summit, sealed until the well, the schedule and the bell are found). Tileset: air-lift vents, live cables, seeding slurry and broken grating; terrace planters, slurry drums, drone netting and antenna masts; a pale sky beyond the edges. New ally: **Dust** (met at the farmer's shelter; Tianxia/PARSU affinity; link: the wind never pushes you). Six signature implants.
- **The Corridor, the finale** (`map-corridor.json`, 34 rooms: the loading dock, the server halls, the maintenance bays and the launch yard; rules: orders and machine hosts; no ally). Cast: Maintenance Quadruped, Drone Swarm, Gantry Unit, Unlogged Technician, Corridor Operator, the Academy Graduate, Kizuna Auditor and Ghost-MIL Infiltrator in rotation; elites the Mediation Clerk (5), Unregistered Operator (15) and Rack Warden (25); **the Lead Operator** on 10; bosses the Signatory (20) and the Pass (30, the launch yard, sealed until the fault, the first copy and the window are found). Tileset: focused light (burns), live cables, coolant and open floor panels; server racks, canisters, terminals and frame crates; black pits beyond the edges. Five signature implants.
- **Endings:** the last chapter's map can list `endings` (2–4 choices, each with its text). Its victory screen asks "What do you do with it?" and shows the chosen text. The Corridor's three are placeholders: go back down into bodies, stay in the machines, let yourself spread. Each map can also set `endTitle`, the headline when its chapter is done.
- **Set pieces** (threat 10) are named by enemy in the cast: the Patchwork King and the Echo are built specially; anything else is that enemy made a wall with the King's multipliers (`createSetPiece`).
- Ally strength comes from `unlockLevel` (stats of a regular enemy at that level): set it to the threat where the ally is met. Old saves' unlock-by-level upgrade only knows the first five allies.
- Each threat level means the same strength in every chapter; a chapter feels different through its cast, board rule and map, and the signal arrives stronger through its tree and allies.

## 8. Runs, seeds and saves

- Save version 4 added the map's chapter (older saves are in chapter one); version 5 added the last body's implants. Version 17: chapter one ends at the King; a host in a room past him wakes at the dock (or the weigh-station, if the King was beaten, which finishes the chapter), and the weigh-station holds the server.
- Every run has a seed. Each fight is seeded from the run seed and the fight number, and each market visit from the run seed and its visit key, so the same seed and choices replay exactly (`client/src/lib/engine/rng.ts`).
- One versioned save holds everything (`client/src/lib/save.ts`). Players can copy it as text and load it back in from the Status tab.

## 8b. Sound

**Where:** `client/src/lib/audio/sfx.ts`, `client/src/lib/stores/useAudio.tsx`.

- Every effect is synthesised in the browser (square and triangle blips, filtered noise), in keeping with the pixel art; there are no effect files. A slow sea wash plays under the music.
- The fight engine marks moments with sound-only events (`collide`, `burn`, `telegraph`, `surge`, plus `hit`, takedowns, an ally down, a chain action and the host lost). The combat store maps them to effects; moving, searching a cache and walking through a door have their own.
- Sound starts off, since browsers only allow audio after a tap; the sound button is that tap.

## 9. Checks that guard these rules

`npm run verify` runs before every deploy:
- **Typecheck.**
- **Content check** (`scripts/check-content.ts`): schemas, allowed values, ids, cross-references, the ally roster.
- **Tests** (`tests/`): the rules on this page, including rarity bands, structure, health floor, dodging heavies, heat damage, the play-i tree, allies and save round trips.
- **Balance simulator** (`scripts/sim.ts --check`): seeded bot runs. New players must die on enemy 2 or 3 in 50–95% of runs, and a first life must win 5 fights in under 60% of runs (both chapter one only; `SMART=1` plays a careful bot that never stands in the red, to check the first life holds against kiting), win rates per threat band must be at least 65%, and the median best run must reach threat 9, regular fights must average 8 turns or fewer and bosses 16 or fewer, and the deepest win must be 20–60 after 10 hosts lost (depth: chapter one is 1–10, chapter two 11–40, and on; the bot goes on to the next chapter when one is done). Firmware (chapter one): its first boss must first fall on host 2–5 (median), the King at least a host after that, and at threats 6–9 hosts with firmware must win at least 10 points more often than hosts without. `RUSH=1` plays a bot that heads straight for the next boss; `NOFW=1` one that never loads firmware; `BYCLASS=1` prints each firmware's record. Win rates are compared rounded, as printed. It runs 20 seeded runs of 250 fights (`CHAPTER=deltas`, `helix-hills`, `rain-machine` or `corridor` plays that chapter's map from a fresh start: all checks pass there too; with the alarm on, the bot prefers tiles beside a cold pod, and it passes on the Reflex step); the bot aims with the game's own rules, prefers a tile outside the red it can still attack from, and avoids parting blows (both with its skill). Between fights it gives spare implants to allies riding along, best fit first. Turn order comes from the store, so the bot plays speed rounds like a player. With no fight left in reach the bot goes and looks at anything it hasn't (that's where the goals are); the report counts runs that beat the last boss. The best-level median is noisy at 20 runs (one run spans roughly 17–26); `RUNS=60` gives a steadier read.
